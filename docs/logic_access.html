Access

For everyone: who may do what, in one place, across the logic panel and the three built admin screens.

Principles a1

This is the access write-up asked for on the 7 Oct 2026 call ("what kind of roles, what kind of access, what kind of permissions"), across the logic panel and the three built admin screens.

A few people (7 Oct 2026 call): two operating seats, the Principal officer and the Logic analyst, plus compliance and read only. At launch two operators, one of whom can publish.

Fixed seats: ten seats, none user-defined. A staff member holds one or more of them; there is no exception for one person.

Two people on every release: the author cannot review, and only the Principal officer publishes; nobody is both on one release, whatever seats they hold. The one exception is an urgent Hold by the Principal officer alone, ratified by a second person within two working days (part 5).

The server decides, the screen only mirrors: the greyed control is never the control. The server checks the seat on every request and every write.

Everything written down: an audit row for every write and every refused write. Releases and snapshots are append-only; a correction is a new record.

The seats a2

Principal officer: publishes, schedules, pauses, withdraws and starts a correction; reviews a draft as the second person, never their own; may place an urgent Hold alone; holds staff and seats and runs the access review; marks a calendar item reviewed, no change.

Logic analyst: the editor of the logic values; opens and edits a draft, runs validation and preview, proposes a route; reviews a draft as the second person, never their own; requests an urgent Hold and ratifies one; drafts the classification of an unknown ISIN.

Compliance: acknowledges an assumptions or a risk scoring release before publish (rule 14) and is told of every other release as it is published, and of an urgent Hold at once; exports the registers, reads the audit rows, adds incident entries; acknowledges an operating seat (LQ42).

Adviser: takes calls; reads the live logic and its rationale, and the plan history of its own people (L15).

Call centre: works the call tasks; reads the live logic and its rationale, and the plan history of its own people (L15).

Ops: adds facts to the unknown-ISIN queue (L04b) and queues the letter for a release in I14 Zoho One; reads the live logic, the release monitor and the client plan history, with client names masked on the monitor.

Support: answers tickets; reads the live logic and its rationale, and the client plan history (L15).

Read only: product and tech sit here; reads the live logic, drafts, previews, the release monitor and the audit rows, with client names masked; writes nothing.

Marketing: no access to the logic panel; its rights on the admin screens are in part 4.

Finance: no access to the logic panel; its rights on the admin screens are in part 4.

An operating seat (Logic analyst or Principal officer) takes effect when compliance acknowledges it (LQ42).

In the examples staff are labelled by seat (Principal officer 01, Logic analyst 01 and so on); no real person is drawn.

Draft states: open -> validated -> previewed -> in review -> approved (or sent back -> open) -> published; or discarded.

Who may author: the Logic analyst or the Principal officer. Who may review: the second person, a Principal officer or Logic analyst who is not the author. Who acknowledges: Compliance, for an assumptions or a risk scoring release (rule 14); LQ45 holds which other releases need it. Who publishes or schedules: the Principal officer only, after a fresh second-factor check.

The review covers the items, the route and the client reason; a change to any of them after approval returns the draft to review.

The urgent path: an urgent Hold by the Principal officer alone collapses steps 1 to 8 into one action. It may carry the sleeve weights the rescale needs to pass L09 and nothing else; compliance is told at once; a second person, not the one who placed it, ratifies it within two working days. Unratified, it stays in force and shows as overdue on L08, L10 and L11 (LQ30). The Logic analyst may only request one (LQ25).

How many people (7 Oct 2026 call): at launch two operators, one of whom can publish, plus compliance and read only. The two-person rule needs both operators for every release except an urgent Hold; LQ7 holds what happens when the publisher is away.

Audit rows: who, seat, when, what, before, after, reason; a refused attempt is a row too, marked (L08).

The Principal officer and Compliance read and export them; Read only reads them; on L08 the Logic analyst sees the register and the versions in use, not the audit rows.

Masking: the Logic analyst and Read only see named client rows masked in previews and the monitor, and Ops on the monitor; an unmask by the Logic analyst needs a typed reason and is logged.

The access review: each quarter the Principal officer goes through every staff row on L13 (seats, status: active, suspended or revoked, added by and when, last sign-in, second factor on) and keeps, changes or removes it. At launch a list and the audit rows serve; the screen can follow.

In the examples, access review 1 on 15 Jul 2027 suspended Adviser 06, idle 90 days.

Session rules are open: the idle timeout, one session at a time, the age of the second-factor check (LQ41).

The app a8

The app has one kind of user. What a client can do differs by tier, which is entitlement and already on each screen of the board (LQ40).

No logic endpoint answers a client's token: the panel is a separate staff web app, desktop only (L01 on the board), kept apart by routing and the token type.

No staff identity is shared with a client (LQ9).
