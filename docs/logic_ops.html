Ops

For the Ops seat (Kajal's seat): what each kind of change sends to clients, the call centre and support, and what ops does before, on the day and after.

What each kind of change sets in motion o1

One row per kind of change and its route; the routes are proposed defaults. A push rebuilds every plan it selects; a client is messaged only at review or urgent size.

Sizes in plain words: a note changes nothing for the client; an outlook changes no instalment but moves how far a goal is funded, or a goal's portfolio, and asks for nothing; a review changes an instalment or the risk band and needs the client's accept; urgent is a Hold marked urgent, or an Exit, on a fund the client runs a SIP into (Hold) or holds on verified holdings (Exit).

Wave plans (rehearsal values; LQ14): an urgent Hold and a correction go to everyone at once; a routine Hold, 200 clients an hour from 10:00 to 18:00 IST; an Exit, even daily batches over five working days.

Figures are typed examples per 1,000 plan holders, illustrative, not evidence; N marks a figure no example gives.

L and V screens are on the Logic tab; Y01 to Y04 are the walkthroughs on the Walkthroughs page.

Before a release goes out o2

Before a release goes out, ops looks down the list for its route. If something is not in place, note it in the box below and we look at it together.

A route uses only the checks it needs: nothing is pushed on the quiet route, and an urgent Hold or a correction goes at once, so there is no day to choose (logic plan, 8 Oct 2026).

Market inputs, guards and constraints and configuration on M10 send nothing to clients, and the route of a new engine version is open (LQ46), so none of them has a list here.

The letter o4

The letter is one email per house-view release that changes what clients see. It says what changed, why, and that the client's plan takes it up at their next review, with the date (V05 on the Logic tab).

It is copy slots, not copy; the slots live with the other message slots (part 5).

Ops queues it in I14 Zoho One; L06 shows the Principal officer, at publish, whether it is queued.

When it is sent is open (LQ33). In the example year the letter for REL12 went out on 1 Apr 2028 at 09:00, queued by ops (Y01 on the Walkthroughs page).

The unknown-ISIN queue (L04b) o6

Some holdings on a client's statement carry an ISIN that the instrument master (I05 Accord) does not know. Each waits in the queue on L04b with its ISIN, the name on the statement, the holders, the facts ops added and the proposed class.

Home (L01) shows ops the queue items that need facts.

In the example year two items waited, one worth Rs 80,000 bought Mar 2024 and one worth Rs 50,000 bought Aug 2023; REL03 classified them on 16 Jun 2027, one known and not approved, one legacy hold.

suspendedPrincipal officerL13the session ends at once; the server checks the seat on every request (LQ41)
removedPrincipal officerL13the seat is revoked; the session ends at once

Seat changes are made on L13. The staff allowlist sits on M03, as decided on 7 Oct 2026; where it lives is open (LQ9).

Seats are fixed: ten of them, none user-defined, and one person may hold more than one. The Access page has what each seat may do.

Adding, changing, suspending and removing a seat each ask for a fresh second-factor check, and each is an audit row.

The access review: each quarter the Principal officer goes through every staff row on L13 and keeps, changes or removes it. It looks at each row's seats, status (active, suspended, revoked), who added it and when, the last sign-in and whether the second factor is on.

In the example year, access review 1 on 15 Jul 2027 suspended Adviser 06, idle 90 days; Logic analyst 02 was added on 4 Oct 2027, acknowledged by compliance, and revoked on 27 Oct 2027.
